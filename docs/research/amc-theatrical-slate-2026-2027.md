# AMC Theatrical Slate Research Monitor: August 2026–December 2027

**Company:** AMC Entertainment Holdings, Inc. (`AMC`)
**Compiled:** August 2, 2026
**Coverage:** Announced North American wide and event-theatrical releases from August 2026 through December 2027
**Status:** Living research document; release dates and forecasts should be refreshed weekly
**Purpose:** Track the quantity, timing, audience breadth, premium-format potential, and preliminary box-office expectations of films that could affect AMC attendance, admissions revenue, food and beverage revenue, and premium large-format utilization.

> This is research and scenario analysis, not investment advice. Film-level ranges beyond published short-term tracking are Learn What You Own research scenarios—not studio guidance or Wall Street consensus. Release dates can move, and box-office outcomes have unusually wide error bands.

## Executive summary

The theatrical backdrop is constructive but not risk-free. Gower Street Analytics revised its 2026 forecast to **$34.7 billion global box office** and **$9.75 billion domestic box office**, still implying approximately 3% global and 10% domestic growth over 2025. Through the first half of 2026, domestic box office reached roughly **$4.8 billion**, 15% ahead of the prior-year period, while a broader mix of films—not only one or two tentpoles—supported the recovery.[1][2]

For AMC, the strongest remaining 2026 concentration is the holiday corridor. `Avengers: Doomsday`, `Dune: Part Three`, `The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping`, `Jumanji: Open World`, and `The Angry Birds Movie 3` provide franchise demand across superhero, science-fiction, young-adult, family, and action audiences.[3] The same calendar also creates collision risk: `Avengers: Doomsday` and `Dune: Part Three` are currently dated for the same December 18 weekend, while several family and adult titles cluster around Christmas.[3]

The 2027 calendar is earlier-stage and therefore less reliable, but it already contains a strong succession of event titles: `Sonic the Hedgehog 4`, `Godzilla x Kong: Supernova`, `The Legend of Zelda`, `Star Wars: Starfighter`, `How to Train Your Dragon 2`, `Spider-Man: Beyond the Spider-Verse`, `Shrek 5`, `Man of Tomorrow`, `A Minecraft Movie Squared`, `Frozen III`, `Avengers: Secret Wars`, and `The Lord of the Rings: The Hunt for Gollum`.[4] This breadth matters: AMC’s filings identify admissions and food-and-beverage sales as its primary theatrical revenues, while management emphasizes premium large-format investment and reports that attendance and ticket-price growth benefit from 3D, IMAX, and other PLF engagement.[5][6]

The key investor conclusion is not simply “more blockbusters.” The value of the slate depends on **calendar continuity**, **film diversity**, **premium-format availability**, and **the absence of disruptive date shifts**. A full year with repeated commercial releases can support attendance and concessions more reliably than a year dominated by a few isolated hits.

## How to read the expectations

Published industry tracking is used only when a reputable tracker has issued a current range. For films too far from release for credible tracking, this document assigns a preliminary scenario band. The band is an analytical screen—not a prediction.

| Scenario band | Preliminary domestic total | Preliminary worldwide total | Interpretation for AMC research |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Event | $300M+ | $750M+ | Likely major attendance and PLF driver; high merchandise and concession opportunity |
| Major | $150M–$350M | $350M–$900M | Broad commercial draw; meaningful national screen allocation |
| Commercial | $60M–$175M | $125M–$450M | Supports weekly breadth and audience segmentation |
| Genre / specialty | $20M–$100M | $40M–$250M | Lower absolute gross but can be profitable and fill off-peak screens |
| Limited event | Not normalized | Not normalized | Engagement value depends on footprint, run length, and ticket premium |

Scenario bands are deliberately broad. They should be replaced by published pre-release tracking when available, normally within the final four to eight weeks before release.

## AMC-specific interpretation

### Why the slate can matter

AMC states that its theatrical exhibition revenue is generated primarily from box-office admissions and food-and-beverage sales.[5] Attendance therefore creates two monetization opportunities: the ticket and the in-theatre spend. AMC’s 2025 Form 10-K also describes investment in IMAX, Dolby Cinema, PRIME, and proprietary extra-large formats, and notes that AMC was the largest U.S. IMAX exhibitor by market share at year-end 2025.[5]

The May 2026 quarterly filing says higher average ticket price benefited from attendance in 3D, IMAX, and other PLF screens, while attendance increased because of stronger film product.[6] That makes PLF-suitable films particularly relevant—but it does not mean a film’s industry gross translates directly into AMC revenue. AMC’s share depends on its geographic footprint, screen allocation, contractual film-rental terms, pricing, attendance mix, concessions, and foreign exchange.

### Positive indicators to monitor

1. **Calendar stability:** fewer release delays and fewer empty weekends.
2. **Breadth:** several films above $30M monthly contribution, not one isolated blockbuster.
3. **PLF utilization:** sustained IMAX, Dolby, XL, 3D, and event-screen demand.
4. **Family attendance:** family films can support group ticket purchases and concessions.
5. **Adult counterprogramming:** comedies, horror, faith, and prestige releases reduce dependence on superhero and family franchises.
6. **Theatrical windows:** longer exclusive windows strengthen the value of cinema attendance.
7. **International balance:** AMC operates in U.S. and European markets; title strength and release timing can differ by territory.

### Risks and thesis breakers

1. Major 2026 holiday or 2027 tentpoles move out of the calendar.
2. Same-date event films cannibalize PLF screens or force suboptimal allocations.
3. Domestic box office falls materially below the $9.75B 2026 forecast despite a strong slate.
4. Attendance growth does not translate into cash generation because film rent, labor, occupancy, interest, or other costs absorb the benefit.
5. Consumers wait for streaming because theatrical windows shorten.
6. Franchise fatigue lowers openings and weakens repeat attendance.
7. AMC cannot fund or capture the expected return on premium-format investment.

## Gaps and uncertainty

- Credible film-level box-office tracking generally does not exist 12–18 months before release. The 2027 expectation bands are scenario classifications, not consensus estimates.
- Several 2027 dates are untitled studio placeholders and are excluded from the priority table.
- The industry calendars occasionally conflict. Cinema United is preferred for expected 2,000+ North American wide releases; The Numbers adds broader wide, IMAX, limited, and event detail.
- The Ankler’s studio slate research is useful context but much of the title-level material is subscriber-only. This document cites only publicly visible descriptions and does not reproduce paywalled content.[10]
- No public source provides a reliable title-by-title estimate of AMC’s revenue share. Industry gross should not be converted mechanically into AMC revenue.
